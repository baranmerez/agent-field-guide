Getting started

Agent=Model+Harness

This whole course exists to unpack that equation. By the end, when you work with Claude Code or Codex, you will be able to tell whether a behavior comes from the model, from the harness or from the context. That distinction is the key to debugging, to writing good instructions and to building safe systems.

A new piece has landed in the middle of what you already know

If you have been building software for years, most of the concepts in this course will feel familiar: client-server, stateless APIs, sandboxes, the principle of least privilege, event-driven hooks, CI. What is new is that a probabilistic component now sits in the middle of all of them. When you call a deterministic function, you expect the same output for the same input; here you don’t. That one difference shifts every engineering habit a little.

So the course makes the same move at every level: first it shows the mechanism, then it ties it to a concept you already know with “you already know this; here is what is different.” You will find these bridges in the blue boxes.

You already know thisAn LLM API is a stateless HTTP service. You resend the entire conversation history with every request; the server keeps no session for you. In level 2 we will trace, step by step, what that means for context, cost and “forgetting.”

Separating signal from noise

Some of the tricks sold under the “prompt engineering” banner really do work; most are folklore left over from older models. This course tells them apart by starting from the mechanism. Claims we call myths appear in the yellow boxes, together with the evidence.

Myth“Tell the model ‘you are the best programmer in the world’ and it will write better code.” On current models the effect is either nil or inconsistent. What works is not a role but context: which repo, which constraints, what success looks like. Covered in detail in level 3.

Three layers, one color code

The colors never change throughout the course. If you see purple in a diagram, the model is speaking; if you see teal, it is the harness.

Model

Trained, frozen weights. All it does is look at the text it is given and produce probabilities for the next token.

Harness

The software around the model: the loop, tools, permissions, sandbox, hooks, context management, the interface.

Context and tokens

Everything the model sees at that moment. That is the model’s entire world; it does not know what it does not see.

Practice

Commands, settings files, real workflows. Things you will use tomorrow morning.

Risk

Security holes, failure modes, mistakes that cost money and data.

Key takeaways

  • An agent is the sum of a model and the software that runs it in a loop (the harness).
  • To fix a problem, first find which layer it comes from: model, harness or context.
  • This field changes within months. The concepts last; for command names and settings, the official documentation is the authority.

How the levels fit together

Levels 0–2 cover the model itself: what it is, how it was trained and what it sees. Levels 3–5 cover talking to the model and putting it in a loop. Levels 6–7 introduce the harness and compare the two big ecosystems, Claude and Codex, both as models and as tools. Levels 8–10 are practical and advanced: really using Claude Code, managing context, automation, and designing your own harness safely.

Don’t just read: poke at it

Each level is split into a few topic tabs. You pick the level from the top strip and the topic from the strip below it. The buttons at the bottom of the page take you to the next topic, or to the next level if you are on the last topic. When the topic strip has focus, the ← and → keys work too.

Every topic has at least one interactive diagram. Play them, step through them, drag the sliders. They are not decoration: they are usually the part of the text that sticks. The diagrams run entirely in your browser and send no data anywhere.

Terms with a dotted underline

Hover over them or focus them with Tab to see a short definition. The Glossary button at the top opens all terms in a searchable list.

Progress ring

The topics you open are remembered in your browser. You pick up where you left off; once you have opened every topic of a level, a ✓ appears on its tab.

Sharing links

A link like #L5/3 in the address bar opens that topic of that level directly. Use it to point a teammate at a specific spot.
